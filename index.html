The approach

A website should make the business easier to trust.

The goal is not decoration. It is a clearer offer, stronger visual credibility, better information architecture, and fewer reasons for a potential customer to hesitate.

01 / POSITIONING

Look established before the first conversation.

Your website sets expectations before a customer ever calls. The visual system, copy hierarchy, service structure, and proof should reinforce that you are capable and worth contacting.

02 / CLARITY

Make the next step obvious.

Customers should understand what you do, who it is for, why they should trust you, and how to contact you without digging through a confusing site.

03 / MOBILE

Designed for the screen people actually use.

Mobile is treated as a primary experience—not an afterthought squeezed down from desktop.

04 / HANDOFF

No forced monthly plan.

Once your project launches, the site is yours. If you need changes later, contact me and I can quote them as one-time work.

Services

Build the right site for the stage you are in.

Some businesses need a disciplined focused presence. Others need deeper service architecture, case studies, integrations, or a flagship digital experience. The scope should match the business—not an arbitrary template.

Complete website redesign

A ground-up rethink of an existing site. I keep the useful information, remove weak structure, clarify the message, modernize the visual system, and rebuild the mobile experience.

  • Information architecture and page planning
  • Custom typography and visual direction
  • Responsive desktop/mobile implementation
  • Calls to action, proof, and lead flow

New business website

For a business launching without a strong existing site. The structure is built around your actual services, customer questions, market position, and contact goals.

  • Core business content and page structure
  • Service hierarchy and messaging
  • Phone, contact, and inquiry paths

Service architecture

A generic services block is often not enough. Strong service content creates room to explain what you do, who it is for, what the process looks like, and why your company is the better fit.

Project galleries and case studies

For visual businesses, completed work can be one of the strongest trust signals. I structure proof so it feels valuable and intentional rather than simply uploaded.

Lead-flow design and integrations

Phone, email, forms, quote requests, consultations, scheduling tools, CRMs, review platforms, analytics, and other integrations are placed where they support the buying path.

SEO foundations

Clean page titles, descriptions, semantic structure, crawlable internal links, responsive layouts, and organized service content are built in. Larger ongoing SEO campaigns can be scoped separately.

Post-launch changes

There is no required monthly maintenance plan. If you need content edits, new sections, new services, or design additions later, I can quote the work as a one-time update.

Bonebrake

I started Bonebrake Web Design around a simple observation: a lot of genuinely good businesses are represented online by websites that make them look smaller, older, less organized, or less trustworthy than they actually are.

A website does not need to be loud to fix that. It needs better hierarchy, clearer writing, stronger service structure, disciplined typography, responsive design, good proof, and an obvious way to contact the business.

I work directly on the strategy, visual direction, structure, and build. That keeps communication simple and makes it possible to move quickly without passing a project between account managers, designers, and developers who never talk to each other.

01

Clarity first

If the design makes the business harder to understand, the design is not helping. Hierarchy comes before visual tricks.

02

Earn the attention

Motion, photography, type, and graphic detail should make the brand more memorable—not compete with the message.

03

Respect mobile

The mobile site gets its own spacing, pacing, hierarchy, and contact behavior instead of being treated as compressed desktop.

I am not trying to make every business look like a design agency. I am trying to make every business look like the best version of itself.
