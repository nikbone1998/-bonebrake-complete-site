## Factual website audit

### What the current website already does well

1. It has legitimate trust material: longevity, family ownership, licenses, project photos, testimonials and contact information.
2. It makes the company’s broad construction capabilities visible.
3. It provides an obvious quote/contact path.
4. It contains enough first-party material to create a high-fidelity redesign without inventing a business story.

### Where the website undersells the business

1. **Trust is present but not prioritized.** The strongest story — a second-generation family contractor serving Chicagoland since 1969 — should be a primary positioning asset rather than one fact among many.
2. **The service architecture is weak.** The services page is largely visual tiles/images. A customer comparing contractors receives little structured help deciding which service is relevant, what Dardon handles, and what to do next.
3. **The homepage narrative is broad rather than decisive.** Roofing, construction, remodeling, restoration and development are all important, but they need hierarchy so visitors understand Dardon quickly.
4. **Project proof lacks context.** The gallery has useful photographs but should explain project type/category and connect completed work back to specific services.
5. **Conversion paths can be more intentional.** Call and estimate actions should remain visible as a visitor moves through trust, services and project proof.
6. **Licensing/credibility can work harder.** The license details currently living in footer-style content can become a visible trust layer without making the page look cluttered.
7. **The site can feel more premium without becoming flashy.** The company’s history and project scope support a restrained, established-contractor visual system rather than a generic home-services template.

## Redesign thesis

> Dardon already has the history, licenses, project work and customer proof. The redesign should organize those assets so the digital experience feels as established as the company itself.

The preview should not attempt to replace the entire site. It should demonstrate the highest-value transformation on the homepage and show the proposed service architecture.
